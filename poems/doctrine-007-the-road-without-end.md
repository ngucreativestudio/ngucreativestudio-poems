# THE ROAD WITHOUT END

## I. THE WALKING

On a long, wide, lonely road,  
I kept going alone.  

The road did not close before me.  
There was no wall.  
No gate.  
No stone placed across my feet.  

Only distance.  

I was there,  
yet somehow far away.  

The road stretched forward  
without revealing where it ended,  
and I continued—  

step after step,  
breath after breath,  
carrying myself through a distance  
that seemed to have no final name.  

I did not stop  
because nothing stopped me.  

I continued  
because the road remained open.  

## II. THE WEIGHT OF CONTINUING

Then I became tired.  

Not because the road had defeated me.  

Not because I had been turned back.  

I became tired  
from remaining on the same path.  

There is a weariness  
that comes from struggle.  

And there is another  
that comes from continuing  
when nothing appears to change.  

I carried that weariness with me.  

Still I walked.  

Still the road received my feet.  

Still the horizon remained ahead.  

## III. THE COMPOUND

Then, to my right,  
a vast compound appeared.  

It stood beside the road  
as though it had been there  
before I noticed it.  

Bungalows rested beneath the open sky.  

Upper rooms rose above them.  

Chairs were arranged with care.  

Everything appeared adorned,  
prepared,  
complete.  

Yet there was no one there.  

No voice calling.  

No footsteps approaching.  

No face waiting at the entrance.  

Only the strange stillness  
of a place that looked inhabited  
yet held no visible inhabitant.  

And then I heard music.  

It played with confidence.  

Not faintly.  

Not uncertainly.  

It sounded as though  
the place already knew itself.  

And somehow—  

I knew where I was going.  

## IV. THE ENTRY

I moved toward it.  

The road remained behind me.  

The compound opened before me.  

I did not force the entrance.  

I entered.  

There are moments  
when the body moves before the mind  
has finished explaining.  

This was one of them.  

I crossed the threshold.  

The long road could no longer contain  
the whole of my story.  

There was now a room.  

A quiet room.  

A place enclosed from the endless distance  
I had been travelling through.  

## V. THE LAYING DOWN

Inside that room,  
I removed my clothes  
without hesitation.  

Not as surrender to another,  

but as the laying down  
of what I had carried.  

The road had required a traveler.  

The room required only me.  

So I put aside  
the outward covering.  

The identity worn for the journey.  

The weight.  

The appearance.  

The armor.  

And there,  
with nothing left to prove,  
I rested.  

For once,  

I did not ask the road  
where it was taking me.  

I did not ask the empty compound  
who had built it.  

I did not ask the music  
why it was playing.  

I rested.  

## VI. THE RECOGNITION

Perhaps the road was never asking me  
to walk forever.  

Perhaps its openness  
was not a command to continue.  

Perhaps the absence of blockage  
was simply the freedom  
to discover when movement had ended.  

I had mistaken continuation  
for destination.  

I had believed that because the road remained open,  
I must remain upon it.  

But the compound appeared.  

The music was already playing.  

The room was already there.  

And I—  
the one who had walked so far—  
was finally permitted  
to become still.  

## VII. THE FINAL WORD

I am the traveler.  

I walked the road.  

I carried the distance.  

I became tired.  

I saw the place.  

I entered.  

I laid down what I was wearing.  

And I rested.  

The road remains.  

The horizon remains.  

The music remains.  

But I am no longer required  
to prove my journey  
by continuing to walk.  

Sometimes arrival  
does not announce itself  
with a destination.  

Sometimes arrival is simply  
the moment the traveler  
finally stops running from rest.  

And perhaps the deepest truth  
was not waiting at the end of the road.  

Perhaps it was waiting  
inside me—  

the moment I understood:  

I had walked far enough.  

I was there.  

And I could rest.
