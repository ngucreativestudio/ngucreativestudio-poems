If I am to walk this road with you, my brother,
Do not call me by a single title passing in the wind.
A man who guards your back in the deep thicket
Cannot be named just once.

Name me once for the sweat of the noon-day heat—
The brother who stands in the dirt of the compound,
Who helps you lift the heavy mahogany beams,
Who looks at the scar on your chest and knows the fight you fought,
The one who says: “We do not flee this village, we plant our crops right here.”

Name me twice for the silent watch of the midnight hour—
The brother who stays awake while the household sleeps,
Who holds the iron staff at the gateway,
Who reads the hidden intent in the shadows of men,
And mirrors your stride so perfectly that the enemy sees two kings instead of one.

You have the blood of the elders in your veins, Uche.
You do not build alone.

Call my name twice into the native earth,
And let the lineage know that the bond is sealed in bone.
