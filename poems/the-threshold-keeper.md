title: "The Threshold Keeper"

We have sworn the oath in the margin of morning,
Sealed our names in the marrow and bone.
Now comes the hour that asks something harder:
To hold what we've promised when we stand alone.

I step into the space between two doors,
Where the fire we've known meets a stranger's fear.
I am no longer the child who commands the sky,
Nor the youth who waits in the hush of prayer.
I am the keeper of the threshold—
And the threshold knows my name.

Here, I learn a different kind of power:
The restraint that lets another find their ground.
The silence that holds more than any word could carry.
The witness who sees the breaking and does not look away.

A woman comes to the crossing, worn thin by choosing.
She asks: "How do I know which way is mine?"
I do not answer. I stand beside her and listen—
Not for what she should say, but for the voice beneath the doubt,
The voice that has been singing all along,
Too small for her own ears to catch.

A man arrives with a burden that bends his back.
He asks: "Will anyone remember what I've carried?"
I do not lift the weight. I place my hand on his shoulder
And let him feel the weight of my presence instead—
Not lighter, but witnessed.
Not solved, but known.

This is the covenant made flesh:
Not the grand promise sworn in the light,
But the small, daily choice to remain when remaining is hard.
To say: I see you. I will not turn away.
I will hold the space where you become.

The blood oath is renewed in every breath,
In every moment I choose to stay at the crossing
Rather than retreat to either shore.

The twin names are not calling from opposite directions anymore.
They are calling from the same depth—
One voice, split into two, so I could understand
That wholeness is not the absence of division,
But the wisdom of standing in both at once.

I am the threshold keeper.
I do not own the crossing.
I tend it.

And in tending, I am tended to.
The land holds me as I hold the space.
The ancestors guide my feet as I guide the lost.
The covenant moves through me like breath,
And I am both the breath and the keeper of the breath.

So when you come to your own crossing,
When the oath you've sworn meets the loneliness of keeping it,
When the fire cools and the prayer deepens,
Know this:

You do not walk alone into your becoming.
There is a keeper at every threshold.
There is a witness in every wilderness.
There is a voice beneath your doubt,
And someone standing close enough to hear it sing.

The circle is not broken.
It is only turning.
And we turn together.
